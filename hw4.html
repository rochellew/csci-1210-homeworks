HW4: Trading Card Collection

CSCI-1210 Fall 2026

Design a page of trading cards about anything you like, and style every bit of it yourself with CSS.

Like HW3, this one is open-ended. You pick the topic, the cards, and the look. The instructions below tell you what your page needs to show off, not exactly how to do it. There's no Bootstrap this time — every style on your page comes from CSS that you wrote.

1. Download the starter code

Download the starter code off GitHub and unzip it -- this gives you the project folder you'll build everything in for the rest of these steps.

Make sure that you unzip the folder to /hw4 and store it somewhere that makes sense, right next to your hw3 folder.

2. Pick a theme for your collection

Think Pokémon cards, sports cards, Magic cards -- but about something you care about. A few ideas to get you going:

  • Your pets (or your friends' pets)
  • Characters from a game, show, or book series
  • Snacks, restaurants, or coffee drinks, ranked
  • Athletes, bands, cars, plants, dinosaurs...
  • A set of creatures you make up yourself

Your collection should have at least 6 cards. Each card should have a name, an image, and a bit of info about the thing on it -- stats, a description, fun facts, whatever fits your theme.

3. What you're given

Your project folder already contains a starter template:

  • hw4.html -- a page laid out with <div>s: a header, a nav bar, a content section for your cards, and a footer. It includes one example card to show you how a card can be put together, plus a mystery card (see step 4).
  • css/style.css -- your stylesheet, already linked from hw4.html. It has the required comment block at the top, a small section of provided layout rules that line your cards up in rows, and plenty of room for your own rules.
  • images/ -- put your optimized images here.
Tip — the provided layout rules: By default, <div>s stack on top of each other, which would make your collection one very tall column of cards. The provided section of style.css uses some CSS we haven't covered yet to wrap them into rows instead. You can leave that section alone -- we'll dig into layout like that in CSCI 1720.

4. Cascade Detective 🔍

Before you change anything, open hw4.html in VS Code (not the browser yet!) and find the mystery card. Its background color is set in three different places:

  • In css/style.css (external)
  • In the <style> element in the <head> of hw4.html (embedded)
  • In a style attribute on the card itself (inline)

Your job is to figure out which one wins, and why:

  1. Predict. Read all three rules in the code first. Before you look at the page, write down which color you think the card will be.
  2. Check. Look at the page. Were you right?
  3. Experiment. Delete the inline style attribute, save, and refresh. What color is it now? Then delete the embedded rule, save, and refresh again.
  4. Explain. In css/style.css, right above the #mystery rule, write a CSS comment with your prediction, what actually happened, and why in your own words.
Bonus question: Did the mystery card's text color ever change while you were doing this? Why or why not? Add your answer to the same comment.

When you're done, the mystery card is yours -- restyle it to fit your collection, turn it into a "card back," leave it as-is, or delete it (along with the embedded <style>). Just keep your comment.

5. Find and optimize your images

Find one image for each card. Same as HW3 -- resize/compress them in GIMP before adding them to the project so the page loads quickly, and save them into the images/ folder.

Need a refresher? The HW3 instructions have a section on optimizing images in GIMP.

6. Build your cards (hw4.html)

Use the example card as a guide, but you don't have to copy it exactly -- add, remove, or rearrange whatever you want inside your cards. Also update the page <title>, the header, the nav bar, and the footer so they fit your theme.

Point your nav links at 2–3 real sites related to your theme -- an official site, a wiki, somewhere to watch/read/buy it, etc.

Along the way, use class and id attributes to set yourself up for styling:

Requirement — classes and an id:

Group your cards using classes. What the groups are is up to you -- rarity (common, rare, legendary...), type (fire, water...), team, flavor, anything that makes sense for your collection. At least one card should have more than one class.

Give one element on the page an id and style it so it stands out -- your favorite card, a "card of the day," your header, etc. Remember that an id can only be used once per page.

7. Style your collection (css/style.css)

This is where your page comes to life. All of your styling (other than the Cascade Detective bits) should go in css/style.css. How it looks is completely up to you, but your stylesheet needs to show off the following:

Requirement — what your CSS needs to include:
  • The required comment block at the top of style.css, filled in with your info
  • Comments explaining what the different sections of your CSS are for
  • Rules that use element, class, and id selectors
  • Each card group looks different from the others, based on its class(es)
  • A variety of text properties (font, size, weight, alignment, spacing, etc.)
  • At least one background image somewhere on the page
  • Colors written in all four formats we covered: named, hex, rgb(), and rgba()
Note — relative paths in CSS: A path inside a CSS file is relative to the CSS file, not to the HTML page. Since style.css lives in the css/ folder, a background image in images/ is one folder up and then over: url("../images/your-image.jpg"). The ../ means "go up one folder." If your background image isn't showing up, this is almost always why.

8. Test it

Open hw4.html in your browser and check that all of your images and styles show up. Try resizing the browser window -- your cards should wrap onto new rows instead of running off the side of the page.

About the tools you're using

Linking your stylesheet

hw4.html is already linked to your stylesheet with a <link> tag in the <head>. Since style.css is inside the css folder, the path includes the folder name:

<link rel="stylesheet" href="css/style.css">

If your page suddenly looks completely unstyled, check that this tag is still there and that the path matches where style.css actually lives.

Selector cheat sheet
Selector What it selects
p Every <p> element on the page
.rare Every element with class="rare" (along with any other classes it has)
p.rare Only <p> elements with class="rare" -- more specific
#featured The one element with id="featured"

To give an element more than one class, separate them with spaces: <div class="card rare holo">

Color formats
Format Example Notes
Named rebeccapurple 17 standard names plus ~128 extras
Hex #663399 Two hex digits each for red, green, blue
RGB rgb(102, 51, 153) Each value is 0–255
RGBA rgba(102, 51, 153, 0.5) Last value is opacity, from 0.0 (invisible) to 1.0 (solid)

All four of those are the same purple. Browse more on w3schools: Color Names.

Looking for more CSS properties?

The lecture covered text, background, and color properties, and that's all you need for this assignment. If you want to go further (borders, rounded corners, shadows...), go for it! Just make sure your page still validates.

A good place to look: w3schools: CSS Reference.

Uploading your files (FileZilla → csci1210.com)

Make sure that your work is uploaded to the right place. Your URL should look like the following:

http://USERNAME.www.csci1210.com/homework/hw4/hw4.html

Replace 'USERNAME' with your lowercase ETSU username. If the link doesn't take me to your page, I can't grade it.

Once everything works locally, upload your whole project folder to the class server:

  1. Connect to the server in FileZilla using your course credentials.
  2. Navigate to /homework/ on the remote (right-hand) side.
  3. Either create a folder called hw4 on the remote side OR name your folder on your device hw4 that contains your files and upload the whole folder.
  4. Upload hw4.html, the entire css/ folder, and the entire images/ folder — keep the same folder structure you have locally.
  5. Visit your page at the corresponding csci1210.com URL and check it exactly like you did locally to verify that everything works.
Warning — check the server, not just your computer: A page that works when you double-click hw4.html on your own machine can still break once uploaded, usually because of a relative path issue or a missing file. If your page shows up with no styling at all, the css/ folder probably didn't make it. Always do your final check by visiting the live csci1210.com URL, not just your local file.
